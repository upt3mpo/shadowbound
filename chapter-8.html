Chapter VIII

The Distance Left

The rift closed slowly.

Not with force. Not with resistance. It receded, as if the world itself had decided to stop looking at it. The tear in the earth narrowed, the pressure eased, and the silence that followed felt less like victory and more like something withheld.

Karthain survived.

The walls still stood. The gates still held. The people within them still breathed.

But they did not gather.

Not near him.

Word had spread without being spoken. Those who stood too close felt it. Not pain. Not injury. A thinning. A quiet subtraction that left them weaker without knowing why.

Ilyas did not deny it.

He did not explain it.

He stood alone at the edge of the field where the rift had been and measured the distance between himself and the city.

It was not far.

It was enough.

The Veil did not quiet.

It never would.

It shifted with him, a constant presence beneath thought and breath, reacting to movement, to life, to proximity. The closer he stood to others, the stronger it became.

The more it took.

Ilyas turned away from the walls.

There were other roads.

Other places where the Veil would be needed.

Other choices that would not be choices at all.

He walked until the city became a shape on the horizon, then a line, then nothing.

Only then did the Veil begin to settle.

Not gone.

Never gone.

Just… contained.

He understood, at last, what the cathedral had failed to teach him.

Mercy was not a question of intent.

It was a matter of distance.

He did not pray.

He did not ask for forgiveness.

He walked.

And the world learned, slowly, to keep its distance from him.

Now the world would have to survive him at a distance.

Distance was no longer safety. It was the only mercy he had left to give.

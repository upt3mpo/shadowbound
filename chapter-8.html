Chapter VIII

Aftermath

Karthain survives, but Ilyas learns that saving the world does not mean he can safely remain within it.

Dawn came late to Karthain. When the sun finally rose, it found the city alive in the way a battlefield is alive after the killing stops.

People emerged from cellars, wells, crypts, and collapsed houses. Some wept at the sky. Some searched the dead. Some saw Ilyas standing in the central square and fell to their knees.

He hated them for that. Not because they were wrong. Because they were still close enough to be hurt, and some part of him still wanted to step closer.

High Inquisitor Vareen survived until morning. Ilyas found her sitting against the base of a broken statue, one hand pressed to the wound in her side. She watched him approach with the calm of someone who had already spent her fear.

“Is it done?” she asked.

“Yes.”

“Are you?”

Ilyas did not answer. Vareen smiled faintly. “That is what I thought.”

He could have healed her. Once, the instinct would have moved faster than thought. Even now, he felt the shape of the spell waiting in him. He could close the wound, steady her heart, and pull her back from the edge.

He could also feel the refugees gathering behind him. Children. Mothers. Old men. Survivors with cracked lips and hopeful eyes.

If he reached for Vareen, the Aura would answer. It always answered. Maybe only one person nearby would weaken. Maybe ten. Maybe the child staring at him from behind an overturned cart would simply grow cold and never understand why.

Vareen saw the calculation in his face. She had spent her life asking others to make necessary choices, and now she watched him become one.

“There he is,” she whispered.

Ilyas stepped back. The High Inquisitor closed her eyes, and after a while her breathing stopped.

No one thanked him after that.

A woman approached near the south gate with a wounded boy in her arms. The boy’s leg had been crushed beneath stone. His face was gray with shock, but he was alive. The woman fell to her knees in front of Ilyas and begged without words, lifting the child like an offering.

Ilyas looked at the boy. Then at the crowd behind her. Then at the frost forming along the edges of his own shadow.

He turned and walked away. The woman screamed after him. Others cursed. Someone threw a stone. It struck his shoulder and fell harmlessly to the road.

Ilyas did not look back.

Beyond Karthain, the world was quieter. Fields bent beneath pale wind. Crows avoided the road ahead of him. By noon, the city was only smoke on the horizon.

He no longer sought absolution. He no longer feared condemnation. Good and evil were words for people who could afford company, for those who still believed a clean choice was simply waiting to be found.

He had once believed holiness meant standing close enough to help. Now it meant knowing how far to stay.

Ilyas had saved the world. Now the world would have to survive him at a distance.

Salvation has a radius.

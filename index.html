Character Design Narrative

Aura of Attrition

He began by standing close to the suffering. He ended as the reason no one could stand close to him.

Concept Overview

A support fantasy turned predatory

Ilyas is a Shadowbound character concept built around proximity, attrition, sustain, and moral triage. Light offers control. Shadow offers speed. The closer others stand to him, the more dangerous his power becomes.

Character Fantasy

The Shadowbound

A corrupted holy figure who learns to preserve life by ending threats faster, even when that efficiency begins to harm the people he meant to protect.

Combat Identity

Attrition Caster

Weakens enemies, drains corruption, converts pressure into sustain, and grows stronger when surrounded by collapsing life.

Core Tradeoff

Control or Surrender

Light protects through slower, safer tools. Shadow ends fights quickly, but the Veil does not distinguish between enemy, companion, or bystander.

Chapter I

The Bell That Would Not Ring

Before the Veil, Father Ilyas believed holiness was measured by distance. The righteous did not stand above the suffering. They stood beside it. They entered fever rooms, plague alleys, battlefields, and funeral houses. They put their hands where others would not look, and they trusted the Light to pass through them without judgment.

The Cathedral of Dawn had been built around that belief. Its nave was wide enough for the sick to sleep between the pews. Its doors were never barred, even in winter. Its bells rang every morning before sunrise, not to summon the faithful, but to remind the starving that someone still knew they were alive.

On the morning the bells failed, Ilyas was hearing confession from a woman named Selka, whose son had been bitten by something that came out of the river fog. She had not come to ask forgiveness. She had come to ask whether mercy could still be given to a child who no longer recognized his own mother.

Ilyas had begun to answer when the first scream tore through the cathedral.

It did not sound like fear. Fear was human. This was the sound of a throat being used by something that had never needed one before.

He ran into the nave and found the congregation folding in on itself. Parishioners clawed at their own skin as black veins surfaced beneath their faces. A mason named Jorren slammed his head against a pillar until the stone cracked. Two choirboys tried to hold down Sister Ameline while her spine arched so violently that her heels touched the back of her skull.

At the center of it all stood Selka’s son.

The boy was seven. Ilyas remembered the baptism. He remembered the child laughing because the water had been cold. Now the boy’s jaw hung open too far, and something black and wet moved behind his teeth.

“Father,” the boy said, though three voices spoke with him.

Ilyas lifted his hand and called the Light.

It came, as it always had. Gold fire burst from his palm and struck the child in the chest. For one terrible instant, the boy looked human again. His eyes cleared. His mouth shaped a word that may have been mother.

Then he became ash.

The nave erupted. Some parishioners fled toward the doors. Others fell to their knees and prayed. The corrupted among them moved faster than dying bodies should have moved, hurling themselves at the living with the boneless desperation of drowning men.

Ilyas fought from the aisle to the altar, one burst of Light at a time. He severed corruption from flesh when he could. When he could not, he burned both together. The cathedral filled with heat and screaming. Wax ran down the candles in thick streams. Blood steamed on the marble.

Selka crawled through the chaos, clutching the ash that had been her son. A Husked Devout saw her move and turned.

Ilyas had enough strength to save her or to hold the eastern doors where twenty civilians were trapped behind fallen pews.

He chose the doors.

By the time the bells finally rang, they were not being pulled by hands. They swung in the wind from the broken tower, tolling over a courtyard filled with the dead.

That evening, the survivors called him blessed. They kissed his sleeves. They thanked him for saving the cathedral.

Ilyas looked at the blackened shape of Selka near the altar steps and understood something that would never leave him.

He had always believed mercy meant standing close enough to help.

The Light was merciful. It was never enough.

Opening Encounter

Ashen Cathedral

Enemy Type: Husked Devout

  • Corrupted civilians force the player to choose between cleansing, containment, and mercy kills.
  • Rescue objectives are mutually exclusive, introducing moral triage early.
  • NPC deaths create later reputation and dialogue consequences.
Ability

Radiant Severance

Channel Light in a focused arc. Corrupted targets are purified or destroyed based on corruption severity.

  • High damage to undead and void-touched enemies.
  • Minor recovery pulse to the player or nearby companions.
  • Repeated use applies Exhaustion.

“Mercy is clean only when seen from far away.”

Design Function

Opening Moral Triage

Chapter I establishes the core theme before Shadow enters the kit: even holy power cannot save everyone at once.

Chapter III

The First Borrowing

The first time Ilyas used the Veil, he did not mean to.

Three weeks after the cathedral massacre, the road to Karthain filled with refugees. They came from river villages and mill towns, from farms where livestock had been found standing upright in the fields after death, from hamlets where children woke speaking with voices their parents did not know.

Ilyas walked with them because the road was safer with a priest and because he no longer trusted walls. The Cathedral of Dawn had taught him that holiness could be breached from below.

They were attacked at dusk by Husks wearing the skins of shepherds.

The first wave came out of the wheat. Bodies bent too low to the ground. Mouths full of chaff. Hands opened wide, as if reaching for warmth. The refugees scattered before Ilyas could form a line.

He called the Light.

It answered, but he was tired. Too many blessings. Too many fevers. Too many prayers given to people who needed food more than comfort. Radiant Severance struck the nearest Husk and split its chest with gold fire, but three more climbed over it before the first body finished burning.

A girl fell near the roadside ditch. Her father ran back for her. Ilyas saw the choice forming again: the girl, the father, the fleeing crowd, the line of Husks breaking toward them all.

He reached for more Light and found only exhaustion.

Something beneath his pulse opened its hand.

Ilyas did not cast. He allowed.

Shadow spilled from him in a thin ring, almost invisible against the darkening wheat. It passed through the Husks without impact, through the girl, through her father, through the refugees nearest the road. No one fell. No one screamed. For one breath, nothing happened.

Then the Husks began to slow.

Their limbs dragged. Their jaws slackened. The black veins beneath their skin dimmed, as if ink were being pulled backward through a straw. The girl’s father found the strength to lift her. The refugees nearest Ilyas stopped stumbling. Color returned to faces that had been gray with panic.

Ilyas felt warmth enter his hands.

Not grace. Not blessing. Borrowed life.

He raised both palms and the borrowed warmth became a blade of violet dusk. It cut through the wheat, through the Husks, through the corruption holding them upright. Bodies dropped into the field without flame, without purification, without the dignity of ash.

The refugees cheered because they had survived.

Ilyas looked down and saw blood running from the girl’s nose.

Her father did not notice. He was too busy thanking him.

Ilyas felt the truth before he understood it. The shadow had not only drawn from the Husks. It had brushed everything living nearby. A sliver from each. A breath. A pulse. A little warmth taken without permission.

The closer they stood to him, the more there was to take.

He could have told them then. He could have warned them not to stand too near. He could have explained that his miracle had teeth.

Instead, he wiped the blood from the girl’s face before her father saw it.

That night, he did not sleep. He sat at the edge of the refugee camp and watched the fires burn low. Every person who drifted near him made the Veil stir. Every heartbeat was pressure. Every breath was fuel.

At dawn, the refugees asked him to walk in the center of the column.

Ilyas agreed.

Field Encounter

Road to Karthain

Enemy Type: Wheatfield Husks

  • Enemies pressure civilians from multiple angles rather than attacking the player directly.
  • Refugee positioning determines how risky Shadow-based tools become.
  • The encounter introduces proximity as a power source and liability.
Ability

Grasp of the Veil

Draw vitality from nearby living creatures and convert it into a focused Shadow strike.

  • Nearby living creatures lose a small percentage of health over time.
  • Damage increases based on the number of affected units.
  • Enemy vitality is prioritized, but nearby creatures are not fully excluded.
Shadow Collateral Risk Proximity Scaling
Core Rule

Power Through Proximity

Ilyas becomes stronger when surrounded, but the Veil treats all nearby life as available material. Positioning is not just tactical. It is ethical.

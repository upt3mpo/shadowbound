Chapter IV

The Company That Faded

A climactic descent below the basilica, where Ilyas learns that victory can still leave no survivors.

The Church refused to abandon Ilyas because abandoning him would have required admitting they had lost control of their own miracle.

Instead, they assigned him companions.

Sir Albrecht Vane was first among them, a broad man with a sunburst shield, gray at his temples, and a habit of speaking only when silence became impractical. He did not introduce himself by title. He looked at Ilyas, then at the sealed gate beneath the ruined east transept, and weighed both with the same expression.

Mira was a novice hymnkeeper whose voice could steady frightened soldiers and soothe fevered children. She carried a healer’s satchel at her hip and watched Ilyas the way physicians watched fever: not with fear, but with expectation.

Tovan Vale was a mercenary who claimed he had fought in nine wars, though he was young enough that at least three of them had to be lies. He wore his grin like armor and checked the edge of his sword whenever the silence grew too heavy.

They met Ilyas at the sealed gate beneath the ruined east transept.

“We are not here to watch you,” Albrecht said.

“Of course not,” Tovan added, tightening the strap on his sword belt. “We are here to make sure the holy man does not heroically die before anyone important can take credit.”

Mira gave him a sharp look. “That is not why we are here.”

“It is partly why we are here.”

Ilyas almost smiled. Almost.

Albrecht noticed. “There may be a man under all that ash after all.”

“There was,” Ilyas said.

The captain held his gaze for a moment longer than courtesy required, then nodded toward the gate. “Then let us hope enough of him remains to open that door and close what waits beneath it.”

Their objective was simple in the way doomed things were often simple. Something beneath the basilica was gathering the bodies of the dead and making a choir out of them. Survivors reported hearing hymns from below, sung backward through broken throats. Every hour the sound grew louder. Every hour more corpses disappeared from the grave trenches.

They entered the catacombs at dusk.

The first passage was ordinary enough: old stone, low ceilings, alcoves filled with bones. Then the walls began to change. Mortar softened into dark tissue. Roots as thin as veins threaded through the cracks. When Mira brushed one by accident, it recoiled.

“Something is breathing,” Tovan said.

“Everything here is dead,” Albrecht replied.

Tovan drew his sword. “That has not been as reassuring as you think.”

The first corpse did not rise dramatically. It shifted. A shoulder rolled. A jaw clicked. Its spine bent too far backward, then snapped upright with a wet correction.

Tovan moved before anyone could stop him. He drove his blade through the corpse’s chest and pinned it to the wall.

The corpse looked down at the sword, then at him.

Its ribs opened.

The bones spread like fingers and closed around Tovan’s forearm. He screamed, not from pain at first, but from the insult of being held by something that should not have been strong enough to hold him.

Albrecht slammed his shield into the corpse, crushing it against the wall. Ilyas struck with Light at the same moment, and the thing burst apart in a rain of ash and black fluid.

Tovan staggered back, clutching his arm. Bone-white scratches marked his skin in a perfect ring.

“Still attached,” he said, trying to grin. “Good sign.”

No one answered.

Mira stepped closer to examine the wound, then stopped short. Her eyes moved from Tovan’s arm to Ilyas.

“Did you feel that?” she asked.

“Feel what?” Tovan said.

Mira did not look away from Ilyas. “The air thinned when he cast.”

Albrecht glanced between them. “Is that going to matter?”

“Everything matters down here,” Ilyas said.

“That was not an answer.”

No, Ilyas thought. It was not.

They pushed deeper. At every junction, the backward hymn grew clearer. It had melody but no breath, rhythm but no life. Mira began singing under her breath to counter it. Her hymn put warmth into the air, and for a while the walls stopped pulsing when she passed.

Tovan stayed near her after that, though he pretended it was because the left flank needed watching. Albrecht noticed. Ilyas noticed Albrecht noticing. None of them moved farther away.

“You watch everything,” Ilyas said quietly.

Albrecht kept his shield angled toward the corridor ahead. “That is how people continue breathing.”

“And what do you see?”

“A man who wants to save people,” Albrecht said. “And something standing very close behind him.”

Ilyas had no answer to that either.

Then the catacombs opened.

They found a chamber the size of the cathedral nave, though no such space should have existed beneath the city. Coffins hung from the ceiling by cords of black sinew. Bodies had been arranged along the walls in ascending rows, their mouths sewn open with wire. At the center of the chamber stood Malgeroth, Choir of Rot.

It had no single body. It wore many. Torso upon torso fused around a central column of bone, faces turning in different directions, all singing the same reversed prayer. Its arms were made of arms. Its crown was a ring of infant skulls polished clean.

Mira stopped singing.

Malgeroth sang louder.

The corpses on the walls opened their eyes.

Albrecht moved first. He planted his shield and shouted for the others to form behind him. Tovan took the left flank, bleeding arm held close but sword ready. Mira recovered enough to begin a hymn of binding, her voice fragile at first, then stronger as Light gathered around her like dawn through fog.

Ilyas stood at the rear and cast Radiant Severance into the first wave.

It worked. For a time, it worked beautifully.

Light carved through the undead as Albrecht broke their charge. Tovan hacked the legs from anything that slipped around the shield. Mira’s hymn slowed the Rot spreading across the floor, forcing it back in golden ripples. They were four bodies moving toward one purpose. For the first time since the cathedral fell, Ilyas remembered what faith had felt like before it became arithmetic.

Then Malgeroth changed the song.

The hymn became a wet grinding sound. The Rot surged across the chamber and climbed Albrecht’s shield. It crawled over Tovan’s boots. It entered Mira’s voice and bent one note sour.

Albrecht grunted as black lines spread up his wrist.

“Cleanse!” he shouted.

Ilyas turned the Light on him. Gold flame burned the corruption away, but the effort cost time. In that time, three corpses reached Mira. Tovan intercepted two. The third struck her across the face and sent her to the ground.

Ilyas burned the third. Albrecht nearly fell while he did.

The chamber forced a rhythm on them. Cleanse Albrecht, save Mira. Save Mira, lose ground. Burn the dead, let the Rot spread. Stop the Rot, let Malgeroth sing.

They were not losing because they lacked courage.

They were losing because courage had become inefficient.

Courage is not a resource. It cannot be optimized.

Ilyas felt the Veil behind his ribs. Not urging. Not whispering. Waiting.

He knew enough by then to understand the risk. Not the shape of it. Not the full cost. But enough to know that anything standing close would be counted.

Mira looked back at him from the floor, blood running from her nose. “Father, please.”

He thought she was asking for help.

Later, he would wonder if she had already understood and was asking him not to.

Ilyas lowered his right hand. It was not surrender. It was a decision made in the instant before shame could argue.

Albrecht saw it. “No.”

Shadow spread from Ilyas’s feet in a perfect circle.

It did not explode. It claimed. The gold light in the room dimmed and inverted. Mira’s hymn twisted into a note too low for human lungs. The undead froze as if listening to a command from beneath the world.

Then they collapsed.

Malgeroth’s many mouths screamed as the Cathedral of Dusk took shape. The bodies fused into its mass shriveled one by one, souls or shadows or whatever remained of them pulled loose in black strands. The Rot recoiled from the floor. Coffins fell from the ceiling and burst open. The chamber became a storm of dust, bone, and devoured song.

For three heartbeats, Ilyas believed he had saved them.

Then Tovan coughed blood across his own hands.

Mira’s hymn stopped.

Albrecht turned from the dying boss and forced his way toward Ilyas. Every step looked impossible. His armor blackened. The sunburst on his shield cracked down the center.

“End it,” Albrecht said.

Ilyas tried.

He reached for the edge of the spell and found none. Cathedral of Dusk was not a cast. It was a condition. Once invited, the Veil did not distinguish between enemy, companion, martyr, or friend. It recognized only nearness.

Tovan fell first. He dropped to one knee, tried to make a joke, and died before finding one.

Mira crawled toward Ilyas. Her eyes were wide, not accusing, only terrified by the betrayal of her own body. Frost whitened her lips.

“Father,” she whispered, “why am I cold?”

Albrecht reached her and tried to lift her away from the circle. His gauntlets passed under her arms. For one moment, it seemed he might succeed.

Then his armor hit the ground empty.

The spell ended when there was nothing left close enough to feed it.

Malgeroth was gone. The Rot was gone. The catacombs were silent.

Ilyas stood untouched at the center of the chamber, surrounded by victory and the exact shape of what it had cost.

Dungeon

Spires Below the Basilica

Boss: Malgeroth, Choir of Rot

  • Rot spreads across the arena and applies stacking decay.
  • The encounter pressures choices between cleansing companions, damaging the boss, and interrupting the choir.
  • Shadow abilities can shortcut mechanics but punish friendly positioning.
Company

The Assigned Companions

  • Albrecht: shieldbearer, captain, final witness.
  • Mira: hymnkeeper and healer, first to sense the cost.
  • Tovan: mercenary, levity masking fear.
Ultimate

Cathedral of Dusk

Create a zone of inverted sanctity. Enemies rapidly decay. Friendly units inside suffer escalating degeneration.

  • Radius expands while enemies are dying inside it.
  • Cannot be canceled once fully manifested.
  • Unlocks Aura of Attrition after companion casualties.

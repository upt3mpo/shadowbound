Chapter IV

The Company That Faded

The adventurers found him two days outside Karthain.

They were not refugees. They were organized. Armed. Intentional. The kind of company that walked toward danger instead of away from it. A shieldbearer, a hunter, a flamecaller, and a field medic who watched Ilyas too closely to trust him.

They asked him to join them. Not as a holy man. As a weapon.

The corruption ahead had grown dense enough to choke the road. Entire caravans had vanished. The company needed something that could thin the numbers faster than steel.

Ilyas agreed.

The first engagement was clean.

The shieldbearer held the line. The hunter broke flanking threats before they formed. The flamecaller burned the field in controlled arcs. Ilyas stood just behind them, where a healer belonged.

When the pressure mounted, he let the Veil open.

The Husks slowed. The company advanced. The fight ended faster than expected.

The field medic said nothing.

The second engagement was easier.

They stood closer to him this time. Not by instruction. By instinct. They felt stronger near him. Faster. More certain.

Ilyas felt it too.

Power came easier when they trusted him.

The third engagement broke them.

It began like the others. Formation. Pressure. Response.

Then the Husks did not stop coming.

The field flooded. Bodies climbed over bodies. The shieldbearer lost ground. The hunter missed a step. The flamecaller burned too wide.

Ilyas stepped forward.

He did not ask them to move back.

He did not tell them what it would cost.

He opened the Veil fully.

The drain was immediate.

The Husks slowed first. Then staggered. Then collapsed.

The company held.

The fight ended.

The shieldbearer dropped to one knee.

The hunter could not draw his bow.

The flamecaller’s fire dimmed to embers.

The field medic looked at Ilyas and understood everything.

They were not losing because they lacked courage. They were losing because every choice cost something else.

Cleanse Albrecht, lose time. Save Mira, lose ground. Burn the dead, let the Rot spread. Stop the Rot, let Malgeroth sing.

“What did you take?” the field medic asked.

Ilyas did not answer.

That night, the company made camp without him.

In the morning, they were gone.

Combat Scenario

Attrition Failure

  • Company positioning increases player power but creates collateral drain.
  • Extended fights create unavoidable companion degradation.
  • Victory conditions conflict with the company’s survival.
Design Function

Collateral Tension

Introduces friction between optimal output and collateral safety. The stronger the player performs, the more strain is placed on companions and nearby friendly units.

Chapter VII

The Veiled Sovereign

Karthain did not look like a city when Ilyas entered it. It looked like a memory being revised by something that did not care if it made sense.

A bridge crossed itself in the air. A fountain poured upward into a blackened basin. Houses leaned together like whispering conspirators. The dead lay in patterns too deliberate to be accidental, spiraling toward the center of the city.

Ilyas walked through it without slowing. Voidborne hunted among the ruins, drawn by the same pressure that pulled everything else toward the Rift. He did not hide.

The first group reached him at the Avenue of Martyrs. Seven shapes, too tall, too fast, joints bending in ways that ignored bone. Ilyas let them close the distance, then opened the Veil.

They collapsed mid-stride. Not burned. Not shattered. Unmade. He did not look back at what remained.

By the time he reached the central square, the rain had stopped falling. Droplets hung motionless in the air, each reflecting a different version of the sky.

In one, the sun still shone. In another, Karthain had never been built. In another, he saw himself standing beside Selka’s son, both of them alive.

He crushed that reflection without breaking stride.

The Veiled Sovereign waited beneath the Rift. It was not monstrous in the way demons were monstrous. It had no stable body. No fixed shape. Limbs formed and unformed around a center too dark to perceive.

When it spoke, every corpse in the square turned toward Ilyas.

“You have carried us carefully,” it said.

“I used what I needed,” Ilyas replied.

“That is what hunger calls restraint.”

The battle began without motion. Pressure struck him hard enough to crack the stones beneath his feet. He answered with Light first. Out of memory, not belief.

Radiance tore through the Sovereign’s form. The wound opened onto something deeper than darkness, and the Sovereign laughed.

Voidborne rose from every corpse in the square. Ilyas moved through them with precision, Light in one hand and Shadow in the other. Every motion was efficient. Every action measured.

It was not enough. For every creature he destroyed, another took its place. For every moment he controlled, the Rift widened.

The Sovereign did not fight like an enemy. It fought like a correction.

It showed him Mira collapsing. Tovan laughing before he failed. Albrecht standing when he should not have been able to stand.

“You think guilt makes you different,” it said. “It does not.”

Ilyas drove his staff into the ground. Light spread outward, forcing space around him. For a breath, the square held still.

Then the Light cracked.

He forced it back together out of habit, out of belief, out of something that refused to let go. For a moment, it held. Then the Veil answered by taking more than he intended.

He understood then that balance was not failing. Balance had never been real.

Balance was never control. It was delay.

The Sovereign leaned closer, its shape resolving just enough to be recognized.

“Stop pretending,” it said.

He tried once more to hold both. Light and Veil, command and surrender, balance forced into existence by will alone.

It tore at him. Not in pain, but in contradiction.

Ilyas let the Light go. Not abandoned. Not consumed. Released.

For the first time, he did not shape the Veil. He stopped resisting it.

Silence spread across the square. The Voidborne folded inward, and the Sovereign recoiled.

Not because it was weaker. Because it recognized what stood before it.

Ilyas stepped forward and placed his hand against the Sovereign’s center. It tried to expand. To escape. To become something too large to be contained.

There was nowhere left to expand to.

The Veil was no longer something he borrowed. It was something he allowed.

He absorbed the Sovereign slowly. The scream lasted long enough for every bell in Karthain to ring once.

Then the Rift closed.

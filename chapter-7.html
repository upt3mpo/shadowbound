Chapter VII

The Cost Chosen

The rift did not close.

It deepened.

What had begun as a wound in the earth became a channel. The unseen pressure that followed the Husks gathered itself, compressing into something that no longer needed distance to cross. It learned the shape of resistance. It adapted to it.

Ilyas felt the change before the next wave formed.

The Veil tightened around his senses, as if anticipating what would be required.

The city behind him held its breath.

This time, stepping away would not be enough.

The pressure advanced without bodies.

It moved through the field like a tide through sand, collapsing space as it passed. Grass blackened. Air warped. The boundary between what lived and what moved became indistinct.

Ilyas understood the equation immediately.

Distance reduced collateral damage.

It also reduced impact.

What approached could not be thinned slowly.

It had to be broken.

He looked back at the walls of Karthain.

Thousands of lives pressed together. Fear. Exhaustion. Hope that had not yet learned it was misplaced.

Power waited there.

Enough to end the wave before it reached them.

Enough to end something else with it.

Ilyas closed his eyes.

For a moment, he tried to remember what restraint felt like.

The memory did not hold.

He stepped backward.

Not toward safety.

Toward the city.

The Veil responded before he reached the gate.

It did not open.

It expanded.

The aura spread through the outer districts first, brushing across the crowd like a second wind. Strength surged through the defenders. The front line steadied. Weapons lifted faster. Feet found ground that had been slipping away.

Then the cost followed.

Subtle at first.

A stumble. A breath drawn too sharply. A hand that failed to close completely around a weapon.

Ilyas did not stop.

He widened the field.

The Veil took more.

The wave met him at the edge of the city.

This time, it broke.

Not slowed.

Not redirected.

Broken.

The pressure collapsed in on itself as the Veil stripped it of momentum, of cohesion, of whatever passed for life within it. The field beyond Karthain fell silent in a way that felt final.

Behind him, the city did not cheer.

People stood where they were, breathing carefully, as if afraid to spend more than they had left.

Ilyas felt the difference immediately.

The cost had been chosen.

Not assigned by circumstance.

Not hidden within urgency.

Chosen.

He did not look back again.

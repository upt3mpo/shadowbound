Chapter V

The Social Cost

By the time Ilyas reached Karthain, people no longer stood close to him by accident.

Word had spread faster than the corruption. A holy man who could end fights quickly. A figure who made enemies falter without flame or steel. Survivors gathered around him not out of faith, but out of calculation.

He did not correct them.

At first, he tried to manage it.

He kept distance where he could. He warned the sick to rest farther from the fire. He stepped back during quiet moments, measuring the pull beneath his skin like a second heartbeat.

It worked. For a time.

Then the city gates closed.

Karthain had become a bottleneck. Refugees pressed against its walls. Supplies ran thin. Patrols failed to return. Every day, more people arrived than the city could feed.

Distance became a luxury no one could afford.

The first time he used the Veil inside the crowd, it felt different.

Not stronger. Cleaner.

There was so much life packed into so little space that the Veil did not need to reach. It simply drew.

The Husks at the perimeter slowed before they reached the barricades. Guards held their lines longer. Fewer people died.

Inside the walls, healers reported fatigue they could not explain.

Ilyas heard the reports. He adjusted his position. He stepped farther from the infirmary. He told himself it was enough.

It was not.

Eventually, it became preference.

Fewer survivors meant fewer variables. Fewer variables meant cleaner outcomes.

He began to stand where the crowd was thickest before each assault. He did not call it preparation. He called it positioning.

The Veil responded eagerly.

Fights ended faster. Lines held longer. Reports of exhaustion increased.

A child fainted near the southern gate during a skirmish. Ilyas saw it happen and did not move.

The Husks fell before they reached her.

The guards cheered.

Ilyas watched the child breathe and measured the exchange without speaking it aloud.

He told himself this was mercy. The relief came easier than it should have.

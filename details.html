Ability & System Details

A deeper look at abilities, traits, tuning levers, item hooks, and encounter behavior for a dark RPG character concept.

Core System

Aura of Attrition

Passive aura that drains a small percentage of health from nearby living units and converts it into damage, sustain, barrier, or resource gain.

Property Value
Radius Variable radius (modified by traits and item effects)
Drain Rate Scales with tuning targets and encounter balance
Targeting Prioritizes enemies, can include companions, bystanders, or friendly NPCs
Conversion Damage, barrier generation, self-sustain, or resource gain
Abilities

Core Kit

Radiant Severance

A safer Light-based tool used to stabilize pressure without escalating collateral drain.

  • Short cooldown ability
  • High burst damage to corrupted enemies
  • Minor recovery pulse to the player or nearby companions
  • Applies Exhaustion on repeated use

Grasp of the Veil

A higher-output Shadow tool that rewards dense positioning while exposing the cost of nearby life as fuel.

  • Moderate cooldown ability
  • Converts nearby vitality into Shadow burst
  • Damage scales with number of affected units
  • Can drain companions or friendly NPCs if target bias is poorly controlled

Veil Expansion

A high-risk escalation tool for resolving dangerous moments at the cost of greater proximity pressure.

  • Long cooldown ability
  • Expands aura radius significantly
  • Increases total drain and output
  • High risk near companions, friendly NPCs, or protected objectives
Traits

Build Directions

Controlled Attrition

  • Reduced companion and friendly NPC drain
  • Smaller aura radius with stronger target bias
  • Safer sustain for longer encounters

Veil Reaper

  • Increased damage against weakened enemies
  • Faster drain ramp during dense enemy pulls
  • Higher risk when fighting near friendly NPCs or companions

Hollow Saint

  • More conversion into barrier and self-healing
  • Lower burst damage, stronger survival profile
  • Rewards careful pacing and enemy grouping
Build Scenarios

How Each Archetype Plays in Practice

Controlled Attrition

Scenario: prolonged boss fight

The player maintains distance from companions throughout the encounter. With a smaller radius and stronger target bias, the aura drains only enemies. Output is steady rather than explosive.

When the boss summons adds, the player repositions to cover them with the aura rather than spiking with Grasp of the Veil. Sustain conversion keeps health stable across a long fight. There are no burst windows, but there is also no collateral pressure on allies and no recovery moments required after overextending.

The tradeoff is visible in this scenario: the player survives cleanly but deals less damage than a Veil Reaper build would during the add phase. The choice between safety and output is made at trait selection, not in the fight.

Veil Reaper

Scenario: dense enemy pack

The player positions at the center of a six-target pack. The aura ramps quickly across all units. At peak drain intensity, with enough stacks built across the pack, the player commits Grasp of the Veil. Damage scales with affected units and delivers a large spike.

The risk is immediate. Any companions inside the radius take strain during the ramp. The player needs to time the burst before repositioning away from allies, or accept that companions will recover on their own while the window closes.

Selka's Blackened Veil amplifies this pattern significantly: each recently drained target increases Grasp's damage, turning the setup phase into a direct multiplier on the payoff. The sustain penalty means the player cannot afford to stay in the pack too long after the burst lands.

Hollow Saint

Scenario: sustained pressure over 30 seconds

The player faces ongoing damage without a clear burst moment to exploit. Aura drain converts continuously to barrier rather than output. Radiant Severance is used for recovery pulses between incoming hits. Grasp of the Veil is held for genuine emergencies rather than offensive windows.

The player survives through conversion efficiency rather than positioning aggression. Kill speed is lower than Veil Reaper in this scenario, but the player is still functional when companions are not present and the aura has fewer targets to pull from.

The design test for this archetype is whether it still requires decisions. Defensive builds should not passively solve encounters. The player still needs to manage when to use Radiant Severance, when to hold barrier, and when the drain cost of proximity outweighs the conversion benefit.

Encounter Design

Behavior Rules

  • Dense enemy packs amplify character power
  • Extended fights increase risk around companions, friendly NPCs, and protected objectives
  • Positioning directly affects power, safety, and pacing
  • Boss encounters should create moments where aura radius, target bias, and timing matter
Itemization

Legendary / Unique Hooks

Reliquary of the Closed Gate

Aura of Attrition no longer drains companions, but its enemy drain ramp is slower.

This item removes the core collateral risk for players who want to operate in party content without managing proximity tension. The tradeoff is meaningful: slower enemy ramp means burst windows take longer to reach, and the ceiling from dense enemy pulls is reduced. The character becomes safer but loses the explosive potential that proximity pressure creates.

Pairs with Controlled Attrition traits. Named after the sealed gate Ilyas found beneath the cathedral in Chapter II, the thing he chose to open. The item is the version of that choice he did not make: bounded, safer, and slower.

Selka’s Blackened Veil

Grasp of the Veil deals increased damage for each recently drained enemy, but reduces sustain conversion.

Creates a setup-payoff loop inside Grasp itself: the player builds drain stacks across the pack first, then commits to the burst for amplified output. The cost is real. Sustain conversion drops, so the character trades self-preservation for damage. Overextending into a dense pull without managing health becomes genuinely dangerous.

Pairs with Veil Reaper traits. Named after the mother Ilyas failed in Chapter I. The item carries the logic of that moment: you gain more from what you drain, but you lose the ability to protect yourself while doing it.

Ash Bell Chime

Radiant Severance briefly marks enemies. Marked enemies feed Aura of Attrition at increased efficiency.

Creates a meaningful bridge between the Light and Shadow tools. Players who alternate between Radiant Severance and the aura are rewarded over players who default to pure Shadow spam. The tradeoff is a cooldown dependency: the efficiency window requires Radiant Severance to be available, and the Exhaustion penalty must be managed across targets rather than stacked on one.

Pairs with hybrid builds across any archetype. Named after the cathedral bells that rang after the Chapter I massacre without anyone pulling them. The item ties the beginning of Ilyas’s story: Light, prayer, the cathedral, to the Shadow system built around it.

Implementation

System Notes

Target Selection

Aura targeting should bias enemies first, then distribute residual drain to companions or nearby friendly units based on proximity and visibility.

Feedback Clarity

Visual and audio feedback should clearly communicate when nearby units are being drained to avoid hidden or confusing penalties.

Scaling Control

Drain scaling should include soft caps to prevent extreme output from very dense encounters.

Risk Signaling

As aura intensity increases, nearby units should show visible signs of strain to reinforce the cost of positioning.

Design Risks

Failure Cases to Avoid

Hidden Penalties

Friendly drain should never feel invisible. Players need clear feedback when nearby units are being affected.

Unfair Companion Harm

Companion risk should create tension, not punishment. Target bias, spacing tools, and trait options should give players ways to manage the cost.

Runaway Pack Scaling

Dense enemy packs should feel rewarding without allowing the character to bypass all encounter pacing or boss mechanics.

Passive Play

Defensive builds should still require movement, timing, and target decisions instead of letting the aura solve encounters by itself.

Tuning

Balance Levers

Aura Radius

Controls risk, positioning pressure, and enemy pack interaction.

Drain Intensity

Adjusts total throughput, survival pressure, and encounter pacing based on design goals.

Conversion Efficiency

Balances damage, barrier generation, self-healing, and resource gain.

Target Bias

Determines enemy, companion, friendly NPC, and objective-adjacent drain priority.

Pack Scaling Cap

Prevents dense enemy pulls from creating runaway output.
